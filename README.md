# Canada Google Earth Flyover

## Planning your route

Choose a route with a guiding question rather than presenting all of Canada or listing cities without a clear connection.

- A list of cities can quickly become a tourist checklist.
- A whole region can be too large and vague.
- A route with a guiding question connects places, geographical ideas and your presentation.

For example:

> **From the Pacific to the Rocky Mountains: How do nature and tourism shape British Columbia and Alberta?**

You could choose places such as Vancouver, Whistler, Banff, Lake Louise and Calgary.

# Assignment: Create a Google Earth Flyover of Canada

## Your task

Create a short **Google Earth flyover presentation about Canada**. Guide your classmates from place to place and explain what can be observed geographically, culturally or historically.

Save your project as a **KML file** ("Datei -> Als KML-Datei exportieren"). Keep it in your Downloads folder and overwrite it with newer versions.

## Aim of the presentation

Your presentation should show:

- where your places are located in Canada,
- why these places are important or interesting,
- which geographical features can be observed,
- how nature, cities, climate, the economy or culture shape Canada.

## Using Google Earth and Street View

In Google Earth (https://earth.google.com), you can:

- search for places and **save them as stations**,
- fly from one station to the next,
- zoom in on individual places,
- explore photos and points of interest (POIs),
- use Street View where it is available,
- export your project as a KML file.

Street View is available in many parts of Canada, especially in larger cities, along many roads and at popular sights. It is not available everywhere, particularly in remote areas, the far north and places far from roads. Look for Street View markers, blue lines or photo spheres in Google Earth.

**Include at least one specific Street View viewpoint in your route.** Mark its exact position in your project and include a link to the view or clearly identify the place and viewpoint. During your presentation, explain what you can observe there and what Street View adds to the satellite view. If Street View is not available anywhere along your route, use a ground-level photo or photo sphere instead and identify its exact location.

## Research your stations

Use Google Earth to explore each place, but do not use it as your only source. Research every station using at least one additional website, such as Wikipedia, an official government or tourism website, a museum or a research institution. Check important facts against reliable sources and list the websites you used at the end of your presentation or in your project.

For each station, prepare brief notes on:

- **Location:** Where is the place?
- **Importance:** Why is it interesting or significant?
- **Geography:** What can you observe about the landscape, city layout, bodies of water, mountains or climate?
- **Visual evidence:** Are there useful photos, POIs or Street View images?
- **Presentation line:** What will you say during the flyover?

Example:

> “We start in Vancouver on Canada's west coast. The city lies between the Pacific Ocean and the Coast Mountains. In Google Earth, we can see its location by the water, its harbour and its proximity to the mountains.”

## Create your Google Earth project

Your project should include:

- stations in a clear and logical order,
- suitable place markers,
- short descriptions for your stations,
- useful zoom levels,
- photos, POIs or Street View where available,
- at least one clearly marked Street View viewpoint,
- a clear starting point and ending point.

Export your project as a **KML file**. Next time you can load the KML file into Google Earth again.

- the Great Lakes
- Niagara Falls

- the Port of Vancouver

## Topic H: Canada's climate and climate change

**Guiding question:** How do climate and climate change affect different parts of Canada?

Possible stations:

- Vancouver or the Pacific coast
- the Prairies
- the Rocky Mountains
- the boreal forest
- the Arctic region
- a community affected by wildfires, melting permafrost or coastal change
- a place where climate change adaptation measures can be observed

Choose reliable sources for current examples. Explain how the effects differ from place to place, and do not present all of Canada as having the same climate or the same challenges.

# Presentation requirements

Your flyover presentation must:

- last **3–5 minutes**,
- include **5–7 stations**,
- answer a clear guiding question,
- explain the places instead of simply showing them,
- include at least **one geographical observation per station**,
- follow a logical route,
- include and discuss at least **one specific Street View viewpoint**; if Street View is unavailable along your route, include and discuss a ground-level photo or photo sphere with its location identified,
- be saved as a working **KML** in your account.

## Speaking notes for each station

Use this structure for each station:

### Station: Place name

- **Where is it?** The place is located …
- **What can you observe?** In Google Earth or Street View, you can see …
- **Why is it important?** The place is significant because …
- **Our comment:** “…”

## Simple Example: Niagara Falls

- **Where is it?** Niagara Falls is on the border between Canada and the United States.
- **What can you observe?** In Google Earth, you can see the river, the waterfalls, the city of Niagara Falls and tourist facilities.
- **Why is it important?** Niagara Falls is one of Canada's best-known attractions and is important for tourism and hydroelectric power.
- **Our comment:** “This is an example of how nature and tourism are closely connected.”
